Double Pendulum (Nonlinear Integrator)

The double pendulum benchmarked with the ShallowNet integrator (see Harmonic Oscillator (Nonlinear Integrator) for the network setup and the regularization sweep). It is built as a two-dimensional lodeproblem; being chaotic and strongly nonlinear, it is a demanding test for the implicit network solve. Its Hamiltonian depends on both q and p, so the energy drift is evaluated from the full state. No closed-form solution exists.

The figures panel by the regularization factor $\lambda$ — a rung of the $\sqrt{\varepsilon(T)}$ ladder, so one panel is a different shift at each precision; solver configurations are on the x-axis and precisions are distinguished by colour. Results are shown at $\Delta t = 0.1$ and repeated for $\Delta t = 1.0$ and $\Delta t = 10.0$ (ten steps each).

using SolverBenchmark

spec = double_pendulum_lode_spec(timespan = (0.0, 1.0), timestep = 0.1)
df   = cached_sweep("nonlinear_double_pendulum_dt0.1") do
    run_nonlinear_benchmark(spec; timing = :quick, max_iterations = 100,
                            verbose = false, quiet = true)
end

Discussion

  • Regularization is not needed here either: at Float64 all four solvers converge at $\lambda = 0$ and at every one of the six rungs, with an energy drift of $2.11 \times 10^{-4}$ that is identical to three significant figures at every value. The solvers differ in cost, not in robustness: Backtracking, StrongWolfe and DogLeg take 3.7–5.5 iterations per step, and Newton/Static 6.8–8.2. The rungs do not change that ordering (3.9–4.4 iterations for the three at $\lambda = 0$, 7.8 for Newton/Static).
  • The chaotic dynamics give a larger energy drift than the integrable examples, and the achievable step size is smaller — at $\Delta t = 1.0$ and above no configuration converges.
  • Only Float64 converges at all (28/28). Float32, Float16 and BFloat16 fail at every rung, and scaling $\lambda$ to the precision does not change that. Float32 stalls on 26 of its 28 rows and raises the OGA initial guess's SingularException on the other 2; Float16 raises that exception, which runs before the Newton iteration regularization_factor acts on, and at BFloat16 the integrator cannot be built — see Harmonic Oscillator (Nonlinear Integrator).
