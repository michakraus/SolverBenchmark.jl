Harmonic Oscillator (Nonlinear Integrator)

This page benchmarks the neural-network variational integrator ShallowNet from NonlinearIntegrators.jl on the harmonic oscillator, built as a Lagrangian problem (lodeproblem). The integrator represents the trajectory over one time step with a one-layer network (S = 4 neurons, activation $x \mapsto \max(0,x)^3$) and enforces the discrete variational principle with an R = 8-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature; the network parameters are seeded with its built-in greedy (OGA1dNormalEquations) initial guess.

Unlike the implicit midpoint analyses, this sweep varies the nonlinear solver's regularization factor $\lambda$ (a Levenberg–Marquardt-style shift added to the Newton Jacobian diagonal) in place of the initial guess, because the network Jacobian is near-singular. NonlinearIntegrators takes the minimum-norm step of a rank-revealing solve, so Newton converges without the shift as well; the sweep measures what the shift costs or buys at each precision. The swept options are:

DimensionValues
PrecisionBFloat16, Float16, Float32, Float64
SolverNewton/Static, Newton/Backtracking, Newton/StrongWolfe, DogLeg
Regularization $\lambda$$0$, and rungs 1–6 of the $\sqrt{\varepsilon(T)}$ ladder

Each run integrates ten time steps, and the three step sizes $\Delta t = 0.1, 1.0, 10.0$ therefore span $(0,1)$, $(0,10)$ and $(0,100)$. The figures below panel by $\lambda$; within each panel the solver configurations are on the x-axis and the precisions are distinguished by colour.

A rung is a different number at each precision

$\lambda$ is swept as multiples of $\sqrt{\varepsilon(T)}$, not as absolute values, so that the shift is scaled to the precision it protects. Rung 4 is $16\sqrt{\varepsilon(T)}$ — 0.5 at Float16, 5.5e-3 at Float32 — and the Float64 ladder is stretched to $2^k$ with $k = 2, 4, \dots, 12$, where rung 2 is the same $16\sqrt\varepsilon$. How the regularization factor scales has the full table; each row's own value is in the results CSV.

The benchmark is regenerated at documentation build time with a single, fast timing pass. See the driver script scripts/nonlinear_harmonic_oscillator.jl for accurate BenchmarkTools measurements.

using SolverBenchmark

spec = harmonic_oscillator_lode_spec(timespan = (0.0, 1.0), timestep = 0.1)
df   = cached_sweep("nonlinear_harmonic_oscillator_dt0.1") do
    run_nonlinear_benchmark(spec; timing = :quick, max_iterations = 100,
                            verbose = false, quiet = true)
end

Discussion

  • Regularization is not needed. At $\lambda = 0$ the Float64 Newton iteration converges for all four solvers in 1.2 iterations per step, with residual $4.4 \times 10^{-14}$ and accuracy $1.44 \times 10^{-13}$. The six rungs, across a ladder spanning $6 \times 10^{-8}$ to $6 \times 10^{-5}$, converge as well and do not improve on it: rungs 1 and 2 take 1.0–1.5 iterations per step, and rungs 3–6 take 2.1–2.4, with residuals of $4 \dots 9 \times 10^{-14}$. The accuracy is $1.41 \dots 1.43 \times 10^{-13}$ at rungs 3–6 ($6.6 \times 10^{-14}$ for DogLeg at rung 3) and $1.1 \times 10^{-14}$ to $9.4 \times 10^{-14}$ at rungs 1 and 2, all at the same order as the energy drift ($3.3 \dots 7.6 \times 10^{-14}$).
  • The choice of line search barely matters: all of Static, Backtracking, StrongWolfe and DogLeg take the same number of iterations at each value of $\lambda$, to within 0.2 per step, because the Newton step is already close to optimal.
  • Both 16-bit formats fail, and regularization cannot reach the reason. No configuration converges at Float16 or BFloat16 (0/28 each), at any rung, and neither failure is in the Newton solve. At BFloat16 the integrator cannot be built: NetworkIntegratorCore reshapes the range 0:1/10:1 into 11 columns, and in BFloat16 that range has 10 elements, so the constructor raises a DimensionMismatch. At Float16 the failure is a SingularException raised in NonlinearIntegrators.initial_params!, by the $G_k x_k = b$ Gram solve of the OGA initial guess, whose selected neurons are linearly dependent at 16 bits. That runs before the Newton iteration of every step, and regularization_factor shifts only the Newton Jacobian diagonal, so no rung of the ladder can lift it. Lifting it would mean regularizing the seed's normal equations upstream.
  • Float32 reaches its residual floor ($\approx 1.9 \times 10^{-5}$) in one iteration per step and is reported as converged under the relaxed tolerance used for this problem ($256\,\varepsilon$); its accuracy against the analytic solution is $4 \dots 7 \times 10^{-7}$. All four solvers converge at $\lambda = 0$ and at every rung, so at this step size Float32 is not regularization-limited at all.
  • The OGA dictionary size (dict_amount) has little effect on accuracy here: a few hundred candidate neurons match the reference's several hundred thousand, while being markedly faster. The dictionary is assembled in double precision, so it neither limits nor rescues the reduced-precision runs.

Coarse time step (Δt = 1.0)

The same benchmark with a ten-times-larger step (still ten steps, so $(0,10)$).

At this step size Float32 shows how indirectly $\lambda$ acts once the seed is the binding constraint: it converges at rung 3 only — and there for all four solvers, with the same residual $4.6 \times 10^{-6}$ — while every other rung, and $\lambda = 0$, raises the OGA SingularException described above. That is not a sweet spot in $\lambda$. The seed is re-run at every step from the previous step's solution, so a different $\lambda$ perturbs the trajectory that the next seed is built from, and which value happens to keep the Gram matrix full-rank for all ten steps is incidental. Read it as "Float32 is marginal here", not as "$8\sqrt\varepsilon$ is optimal here". Float64 converges for all four solvers at $\lambda = 0$ and at every rung, in 3.0–3.8 iterations per step and with the same accuracy $1.53 \times 10^{-6}$.
