Key Findings

A summary of what the two experiment sets actually measured, gathered in one place. Every number below comes from the raw results the per-problem pages regenerate; the figures and tables that support each claim are on those pages.

Measured 2026-11-01 with Julia 1.13.3 against GeometricIntegrators 0.18.7, GeometricIntegratorsBase 0.6.10, SimpleSolvers 0.14.3, GeometricProblems 0.9.2, NonlinearIntegrators 0.5.0 and EulerLagrange 0.5.3. The implicit-midpoint set is twelve sweeps (six problems × two time steps) of 96 runs each; the nonlinear set is twelve sweeps (four problems × three time steps) of 112 runs each (4 precisions × 4 solvers × 7 regularization factors).

Solvers and line searches

  • Newton with a robust line search, and DogLeg, are the most efficient — about 1.4 iterations per step averaged over the oscillator and the pendulum, against 3.2 for Newton/Bisection.

  • All six line searches are comparably robust, and so is DogLeg. Over the twelve implicit-midpoint sweeps, 144 runs each:

    Solver configurationConverged
    Newton/Bisection119 / 144
    Newton/StrongWolfe107 / 144
    Newton/Backtracking106 / 144
    DogLeg106 / 144
    Newton/BierlaireQuadratic105 / 144
    Newton/Static101 / 144
    Newton/Quadratic97 / 144
    Picard1 / 144

    The choice between line searches matters far less than the choice of solver.

  • Picard converges nowhere. Its one converged row is BFloat16 on the Harmonic Oscillator at $\Delta t = 1.0$, inside a tolerance of 0.0625. Of the other 143 rows, 57 raise an exception and 86 run to the end of the time span without meeting the tolerance: at Float64 on the oscillator it takes 2.0 to 2.05 iterations per step and leaves a residual of 2.2e-4 to 0.25. On the Toda Lattice it is the only failure at Float32/Float64, and over all twelve sweeps it is 36 of the 40 failures at Float64.

Reading the residual columns

Bisection stops at the tolerance it was asked for, where the others overshoot it. Its median residual over the converged runs is 0.98 times f_abstol, against 0.60 to 0.88 times f_abstol for the other configurations. This is not a defect — Bisection is also the most robust of the six, so the two facts are related rather than in tension.

Read the residual columns against f_abstol, not against each other. Every results table records the f_abstol each run was solved to and marks rows that stopped at that target in an at_tolerance column.

That column matters most at reduced precision. The Double Pendulum relaxes its tolerance to $256\,\varepsilon(T)$ because its residual floor sits well above $\varepsilon(T)$ — measured, at $\approx 200 \dots 256\,\varepsilon(T)$ at every precision, since the floor is set by the problem's $O(g m l)$ force scale rather than by the format. At BFloat16 that tolerance is 2.0, comparable to the energy itself, so a converged BFloat16 row there reports having met a target too loose to say much.

Precision

  • Precision sets the achievable accuracy. On the oscillator at $\Delta t = 0.1$ the median energy drift is $\approx$ 7e-17, 1.3e-7, 4.4e-4 and 3.9e-3 for Float64, Float32, Float16 and BFloat16, while the discretization error ($\approx$ 1.47e-2 here) is precision-independent — Float32 already reaches it.

  • Over all 288 implicit-midpoint runs at each precision:

    PrecisionConverged

The two 16-bit formats

Float16 and BFloat16 divide the same 16 bits differently — 11 significand bits and a 5-bit exponent against 8 and 8 — so sweeping both separates a failure caused by too few digits from one caused by too little dynamic range. They give two distinct answers, and it is worth keeping them apart.

On conditioning, the answer is "significand, not exponent". Where the comparison is not confounded by the time-grid effect below, BFloat16 never beats Float16: 2/24 against 7/24 on Lotka–Volterra (2d) and 4/24 against 4/24 on Lotka–Volterra (4d) at $\Delta t = 0.1$. The stiff-system failures come from near-singular factorizations, which need significand bits — and BFloat16 trades three of them away for an exponent range these problems never need.

On the time grid, BFloat16 has a limitation Float16 does not. With 8 significand bits its spacing at $t = 100$ is 0.5, five times a $\Delta t = 0.1$ step, so the time series collides: 0:0.1:100 holds 1001 points but only 451 distinct values. HermiteExtrapolation and MidpointExtrapolation are then handed two identical times and abort for every solver, leaving only NoInitialGuess. This is a property of the time grid, not of the solver, and it reverses the usual trend — the oscillator goes from 7/24 at $\Delta t = 0.1$ to 22/24 at $\Delta t = 1.0$, the one place in this study where a larger step helps. Float16, with 11 significand bits, resolves both grids.

Only compare the two formats at a $\Delta t$ where the BFloat16 grid resolves, or the second effect will be mistaken for the first.

`BFloat16` needs a compatibility layer

BFloat16 is not supported by this stack out of the box: Base and BFloat16s together lack rem, Integer, sincos (which recurses until the stack overflows), two-argument atan, fma and mod2pi, and NaNMath — against which GeometricProblems writes its right-hand sides — declares its NaN-returning variants for Union{Float16,Float32,Float64} only. src/bfloat16.jl fills those gaps; it is type piracy over upstream omissions and is meant to be deleted as they are fixed.

Time step

Larger time steps increase iteration counts: at Float32/Float64, Newton takes 2.3 iterations per step on the pendulum at $\Delta t = 0.1$ and 3.4 at $\Delta t = 1.0$, and the Toda lattice, the double pendulum and both Lotka–Volterra systems rise by a similar amount. The linear oscillator is the exception (1.65 against 1.3). At these precisions the convergence count barely moves with the step; the BFloat16 exception above is where a larger step fixes the time-grid collision.

Nonlinear integrator set

  • The Newton solve does not need regularization. NonlinearIntegrators takes the minimum-norm step of a rank-revealing solve, so the near-singular network Jacobian does not stop the iteration. At Float64, $\lambda = 0$ converges wherever the rungs do, but for one marginal row (below): every cell of four solvers is 4/4 at $\lambda = 0$ and at all six rungs on the four problems at $\Delta t = 0.1$ and on the oscillator at $\Delta t = 1.0$.     $\Delta t = 10.0$ converges once in 448 runs. At $\Delta t = 1.0$ only the harmonic oscillator survives, with 32 — 28 of them Float64 at $\lambda = 0$ and every rung, and 4 Float32 at rung 3 alone. On the coarse steps 150 of the 195 failed Float64 rows stall above the tolerance. The other 45 raise an exception: 29 on the double pendulum are a DomainError, raised when its trigonometric right-hand side receives an infinite angle, and 16 are the OGA SingularException described below.

  • Neither 16-bit format converges anywhere in this set — 0/28 in every sweep, for both. Float32 reaches 40/336 and Float64 141/336.

  • The 16-bit formats fail before the Newton solve. Of 336 rows each, BFloat16 and Float16 raise an exception on every one: not a single 16-bit run reaches a Newton iteration that could be reported as non-convergent, so $\lambda$ cannot act on them.

    • At BFloat16 the integrator cannot be built. NetworkIntegratorCore reshapes the range 0:1/10:1 into 11 columns, and in BFloat16 that range has 10 elements, so the constructor raises a DimensionMismatch.
    • At Float16 the exception is a SingularException from NonlinearIntegrators.initial_params! — the $G_k x_k = b$ Gram solve of the OGA initial guess, whose selected neurons are linearly dependent at 16 bits. That runs before the Newton solve of every step, and regularization_factor shifts only the Newton Jacobian, so no rung reaches it. On the double pendulum at $\Delta t = 10.0$ the Float16 rows raise the infinite-angle DomainError instead.

  • Float32 is limited by the same seed, not by the Newton solve. 181 of its 296 failed rows raise the OGA SingularException, and the other 115 stall. Where it converges, $\lambda$ decides it in no consistent way: on the oscillator at $\Delta t = 0.1$ all seven values converge, at $\Delta t = 1.0$ only rung 3, and on the Toda lattice at $\Delta t = 0.1$ only rungs 1 and 6. The OGA guess is rebuilt from the previous step's solution, so $\lambda$ perturbs the trajectory the next seed is built from and decides by chance whether a later step's Gram matrix stays full-rank. Read these isolated survivals as marginality, not as an optimum in $\lambda$.

  • Float32 LODE runs are as fast as Float64 ones (0.0063 s against 0.0067 s on the harmonic oscillator).
